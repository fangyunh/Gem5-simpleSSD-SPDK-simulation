## Writing-style guardrails (apply to every paragraph in `PAPER_DRAFT.md`)

These rules are author-imposed to keep the prose in a recognizable academic register. They apply to the paper draft. Outline notes (this file) may relax them where clarity benefits.

1. Do not use em-dashes "—" as a parenthetical separator. Replace them with full sentences joined by transition words such as "however", "therefore", "moreover", "in particular", "consequently", "rather", or with comma-flanked relative clauses.
2. Do not use colons ":" to introduce lists or examples mid-sentence. Reformulate as two sentences or as a comma-introduced restrictive clause.
3. Do not use parentheses "()" for asides, definitions, or numerical clarifications. Integrate the content into the surrounding sentence, or break it out as a second sentence.
4. Compound modifier hyphens such as "on-die", "100M-IOPS-class", "multi-million" remain in use. The rule above targets the em-dash specifically.
5. Each paragraph in `PAPER_DRAFT.md` is written as a single physical line with no soft wraps, so it copies cleanly into the manuscript.
6. Maintain academic style throughout. Avoid first-person plural rhetorical flourishes unless reporting authorial action ("we measure", "we propose").
7. Avoid hard-to-understand abstractions that obstruct the reader. Replace dense jargon such as "per-I/O floor", "structural ceiling", "execution-truth", "saturation point" with the concrete behavior the term describes, for example "every additional IOPS demanded adds a fixed number of CPU cycles to the I/O path" or "doubling the IOPS demand doubles the CPU cycles consumed by the I/O path". A useful test is whether a reader who has only read §1 can understand the sentence on first pass; if not, rewrite in plainer terms. When a coined term is genuinely needed for the rest of the paper, define it in the same sentence it first appears.

## §1. Introduction

### 1.1 What to write (content brief)

A five-paragraph introduction that argues the thesis: *at multi-million-IOPS scale, the host CPU is the structural bottleneck, and the natural fix is to move per-IO queue execution into the CPU uncore.* Each paragraph plays a specific role in the argument; do not blend roles across paragraphs.

**Paragraph 1 — Device supply trend and the under-explored CPU consumer (~7 sentences, currently realized in `PAPER_DRAFT.md` §1 P1).**
- Open with the AI retrieval workload class. Name billion-scale ANN over NAND-resident indices and retrieval-augmented generation, citing `diskann-github` and `RAGX`. Identify the access pattern as small random reads of 4 to 16 KB at multi-million IOPS rates.
- Anchor the device-side roadmap by framing the push along **two complementary axes**, namely (i) multi-device PCIe aggregation that places many high-IOPS NVMe drives behind a single CPU, and (ii) continuing per-device latency reductions under PCIe Gen5/Gen6 and emerging storage-class memory. This two-axes framing is load-bearing because it forecloses the reviewer attack "your MIOPS regime exists only via parallelism" — both per-device latency reductions and per-server aggregation are real device trajectories.
- Cite Micron's 230M-IOPS single-server demonstration over 44 Gen6 SSDs as the aggregation anchor (`meredith2025micron230m`) and NVIDIA's StorageNext as the framing anchor (`newburn2025storagenext`) that positions this push as a precondition for keeping GenAI workloads fed. Do NOT cite Kioxia FMS 2025 in the draft; the two Micron + NVIDIA anchors are sufficient and the Kioxia cite was dropped during the §1 writing pass.
- Pivot the argument from supply to consumption. State that the open question is no longer whether devices can supply MIOPS but which compute substrate can consume them.
- Acknowledge the GPU-side research line as the studied consumer, citing `NVDIABaM`. Frame this honestly. Streaming-multiprocessor parallelism naturally absorbs the IOPS, yet such designs benefit only GPU-resident consumers and require application rewrites. Do NOT cite `gids_vldb24` in the draft — the BaM cite alone carries this point and the GIDS cite was dropped during the §1 writing pass.
- Close with the gap claim that motivates the paper. The CPU consumption path, which still handles control planes, retrieval orchestration, multi-agent collaboration, and the vast majority of general-purpose I/O, remains structurally under-explored at this regime.

**Paragraph 2 — The host CPU wall (~9 sentences, currently realized in `PAPER_DRAFT.md` §1 P2).**
- Open with the per-I/O cycle-budget framing so the bridge from P1 is not abrupt. At multi-million-IOPS rates, user-space polled frameworks such as SPDK (`Yang2017SPDKAD`) and kernel-side polled paths such as io_uring with SQPOLL (`axboe2019iouring`) already eliminate the syscall, interrupt, and scheduler costs that dominated earlier kernel-driven NVMe, yet a residual per-I/O cost remains that no further driver work can lift.
- Anchor the magnitude with one peer-reviewed measurement. The current draft uses Haas et al. (`Haas2023WhatMN`) and states the framing in compressed form, namely that on real Gen4 NVMe arrays running SPDK, massive available cycles from CPU cores must be dedicated to I/O submission and reaping to sustain tens-of-millions of IOPS for actual application work. The compressed wording was chosen over an explicit "6,500 of 13,000 cycles" quotation so that the prose does not invite the "your single-qpair simulator does not match Haas's 64-core 8-drive setup" rebuttal; the precise ratio is reintroduced inside §4.1 / §4.3 where the simulator setting is also explicit. Do NOT re-introduce the 6,500/13,000 quotation in §1 P2.
- Drop the i10 anchor from §1 P2's prose; the current draft pushes i10 into §2.2 Para A where it is used to argue that kernel optimization does not lift the per-IO floor either (`i10-atc19`). The §1 prose stays focused on the magnitude claim.
- Specific structural-cause claim. The cost is intrinsic to the NVMe queue execution model, in which every I/O requires PRP-list construction from a DRAM-resident SQE, a tracker for the per-I/O command-identifier lifecycle, and a coherence-paying poll on a DMA-invalidated completion cacheline.
- Decompose into two compounding components, namely (i) a control-plane instruction count that no driver can eliminate and (ii) memory-hierarchy and signaling-fabric overhead from DRAM-resident queue metadata, DMA-invalidated completion cachelines, and MMIO ordering serialization. The two-component framing is load-bearing because §3 will eliminate (i) entirely with the mailbox SQ engine and the HW free-CID ring, while (ii) is what motivates uncore placement.
- Close by naming the wall so P3 can refer back to it cleanly. Because both components apply to every I/O and cannot be reduced by software, every additional IOPS demanded adds a fixed number of CPU cycles to the I/O path; at multi-million-IOPS rates this fixed cost grows large enough to consume a majority of the available CPU cycles. This is the **host CPU wall**.
- The simulator-side per-stage decomposition is NOT mentioned in §1 P2; it is deferred to §2.3, which carries the simulation evidence on its own without needing a forward-reference sentence in the introduction.

For NVMeHA / NVMeCHA / DirectNVM the per-I/O queue work is done in FPGA *fabric*, not on any CPU, so there is no "CPU" for the wall to reappear on. The only mode where cost migration is correct is app-on-DPU, and that mode is impractical for general-purpose multi-tenant servers — so the scope-mismatch framing covers all three cases cleanly, while cost-migration covers only one. **§2.1 The NVMe queue execution model (~9 sentences, no figure, currently realized in `PAPER_DRAFT.md` §2.1).**
- Define the data structures and the I/O lifecycle: SQ/CQ ring buffers in host DRAM with head/tail pointers, BAR0-mapped doorbells (`nvme-spec`), 64-byte SQE formatting with PRP/SGL list, SQ tail doorbell, device DMA fetch, command execution, payload DMA, 16-byte CQE write with phase bit, optional MSI-X, host poll or interrupt, CQ head doorbell to release the slot. The draft compresses these into two sentences.
- Identify the **four per-IO recurring costs** explicitly. The labels below are the ones the draft uses and that §3 refers back to by name:
  - **Cost 1 — doorbell traffic.** Each I/O issues an MMIO store to BAR0 that drains through the CPU's posted-write path and cannot be coalesced with prior cacheable stores to the ring.
  - **Cost 2 — completion polling.** Each new CQE the device DMAs into the CQ ring forces the polling cacheline through a coherence transition on the host core, paying an LLC- or memory-side miss per completion at saturation.
  - **Cost 3 — queue-state coherence traffic.** Each I/O writes one SQE cacheline that the device must pull from the host's cache hierarchy and one CQE cacheline that the device writes back into it, paying a cache-line round-trip per I/O regardless of payload size.
  - **Cost 4 — ordering serialization.** The architectural fence between SQE write and doorbell store, and between completion observation and CQ head doorbell update, prevents the CPU from pipelining adjacent submissions or completions through its store buffer.
- Note for downstream sections. The old `(a) UC-WC MMIO / (b) DMA-invalidated polling / (c) DRAM metadata / (d) ordering fences` labels (used in earlier outline drafts) have been **retired**. Use the four names above when referring back from §3.3 (mechanism mapping) and §4.2 (per-stage decomposition).

That structure has been retired and §3 collapses from five subsections to four. Reasons. **§3.1 Placement and principle (~8 sentences).**
- State the placement: an I/O Uncore block (IAU) co-located with the IMC and PCIe root complex on the CPU die, shared across cores within a chiplet (precedent: VMD already lives there for PCIe enumeration; cite `intel_vmd`).
- **State the functional reason placement matters, not only the precedent (one sentence, added 2026-06-07 per item 5).** On-die placement is what lets IAU intercept the per-IO costs before they cross PCIe: the mailbox submission becomes a short on-die MMIO transaction rather than a PCIe posted write to the device (Cost 1, doorbell traffic), and the readiness hint is polled on-die without a PCIe round trip (Cost 2, completion polling). A device-side or DPU-side block cannot remove these costs because it sits on the far side of the very link the costs are paid to traverse. This is the load-bearing "why uncore, not on-device / not on-DPU" argument; §2.2 supplied the scope-mismatch framing, and this sentence supplies the mechanism-level reason.
- State the principle: **architectural truth ≠ execution truth.** Software and the NVMe spec continue to observe DRAM-resident SQ/CQ rings, MSI-X interrupts, and standard head/tail semantics. The uncore privately executes the hot path from SRAM-resident state and updates the DRAM rings *in batches* purely as a compatibility mirror. This is the full IAU design and §3 should present it as such.
- **One sentence on how the compatibility mirror is maintained (added 2026-05-27).** SQ and CQ ring state is reflected into DRAM in batches under a count-or-timer policy, per-IO MMIO doorbells from the uncore to the device are aggregated similarly, and MSI-X interrupts are coalesced so one interrupt covers many completed I/Os. These are properties of the uncore design that follow directly from the principle, not separately evaluated modes.
- **DESIGN vs EVALUATION split (drafter note, added 2026-06-07 per the author's clarification — this is a meta-instruction, not paper prose).** The DRAM compatibility mirror is part of the §3 *design* and stays in §3 in full. *Cross-cutting note (retained from 2026-05-17).* Each engine description names two things together, namely what the engine removes from the host (the NVMe-protocol portion) and what stays on the host as a residual (the application-level portion that cannot move off-CPU without changing application semantics). - *SQ Engine (submission).* Ingests a compact 24-byte descriptor delivered as three sequential 8-byte MMIO writes to the per-queue mailbox region at BAR0+0x3000, and builds the full 64-byte NVMe SQE in SRAM. **Wire format per RTL spec §4.0 (now the standard).** The descriptor carries opcode and flags, the namespace identifier, the starting LBA, the block count, the queue-pair identifier, and the host data-buffer address; the command identifier and the PRP list are deliberately absent because the SQE Engine assigns the CID from an internal per-queue counter and expands the PRP entries on-die (S\_PRP\_SIMPLE / S\_PRP\_DUAL / S\_PRP\_LIST for transfers up to 128 KB). ## §5. Conclusion

### 5.1 What to write (content brief)

One paragraph, ~5 sentences. No new claims, no new figures.
